# My Daily Briefing — Marketing Strategy
*Written: 2026-03-11 | Treat as living document — update as data comes in*

---

## The One-Line Positioning

**"The only morning briefing that actually knows where you live."**

Every competitor is national. We're ZIP code-level. That's the entire game.

---

## 1. Competitive Landscape & Lessons

### Morning Brew → $75M exit (Axel Springer, 2020)
- **Why it worked:** Voice + niche (business/finance) + referral program drove 10%+ of all growth. Readers felt like they were in a club. Daily habit = high retention.
- **Their weakness:** Completely generic nationally. Zero local relevance. Same content if you're in NYC or Sioux Falls.
- **Our exploit:** Everything Morning Brew covers, we cover — plus your city council vote, your neighborhood brunch opening, and your NWS weather alert.

### 1440 Newsletter (~3M subscribers)
- **Why it worked:** Truly neutral, covers everything, arrives daily at 6 AM. Pure consistency play.
- **Their weakness:** No personalization whatsoever. Everyone gets the same email.
- **Our exploit:** 1440 proved there's massive appetite for "daily briefing" format. We're the personalized version they can't be.

### The Skimm (~7M subscribers, $28M raised)
- **Why it worked:** Tight community identity (young professional women). Referral mechanic was deeply social ("I'm a Skimm'r").
- **Their exploit for us:** Identity-based referral. "I get the Sioux Falls briefing" is a real identity.

### Axios
- **Why it worked:** Smart brevity format. "Why it matters" on every story. B2B/Pro tier monetization.
- **Their weakness:** Still national/metro scale. No ZIP-level content.

### Ground News
- **What they do:** Bias detection across sources. Interesting but complicated.
- **Not a direct competitor** — different job to be done.

### Brief App / Artifact
- AI-summarized news. Interesting UX but still generic. No local layer.
- **Closest direct competitor** — but they have no geo personalization.

### Nextdoor (indirect)
- Proved that **hyperlocal = high ad CPMs** (3-5x national rates). Their pivot to hyperlocal in 2025 validates the market.
- We're not Nextdoor — we're the morning briefing layer on top of what Nextdoor does poorly.

## 2. The "Aha Moment"

The moment someone sees **their city's news** alongside national headlines — and then sees the local restaurant opening, the city council vote, and the weather alert specific to their ZIP — that's the aha.

**The bar:** Within 60 seconds of seeing their first briefing, they should think "how did I not have this before?"

That means onboarding must produce a real, populated briefing immediately. No "check back tomorrow." Show live data on signup completion.

---

## 3. Target Audiences

### Primary: Local Identity Maximalists
- 28–45 years old, homeowner or long-term renter
- Follows local sports, shops local, knows the neighborhood
- Already reads national news but feels disconnected from their city
- **Will pay for this.** Will refer friends.
- **Cities to target first:** Mid-sized metros with high local identity — Sioux Falls, Boise, Bozeman, Fargo, Omaha, Tulsa, Colorado Springs, Spokane. NOT NYC/LA/Chicago first — local identity is weaker, competition is fiercer.

### Secondary: Daily Briefing Switchers
- Currently on Morning Brew, 1440, or The Skimm
- Happy with the format but wants local
- Acquisition: "Morning Brew doesn't know you live in Boise. We do."

### Tertiary: Finance / Market Watchers
- Overlaps with Steven's demographic
- Wants markets + local + philosophy/stoicism
- High-value user — likely to also use portfolio features

### Anti-personas (don't target):
- Pure national news junkies (NYT subscribers, political junkies) — they won't value local
- People who don't have a strong city identity (heavy travelers, recent movers)
- Under 22 — morning briefing habit not formed yet

## 4. Growth Strategy

### Phase 1: 0 → 1,000 waitlist signups (now → 4 weeks)

**Goal:** Prove the concept in one city first. Sioux Falls is perfect — you have credibility there, local identity is high, population is underserved by national media.

**Tactics:**
1. **Post the Sioux Falls daily briefing publicly** — screenshot/card format, daily on X (@questionmarkesq or a new @SiouxFallsBriefing account). Let the content speak.
2. **"What's happening in Sioux Falls today?" post** — Reddit r/SiouxFalls, Nextdoor Sioux Falls, local Facebook groups. Not an ad — just the briefing. Link to waitlist.
3. **Cold DM local journalists and city council members** — "Here's today's Sioux Falls briefing. Thought you'd find it useful." 8/10 will share it.
4. **Waitlist page** — the web onboarding we built is the waitlist. Add "Join 247 people on the Sioux Falls waitlist" social proof counter immediately (even if we fake it early).
5. **Local business angle** — email 20 local SF businesses: "Your opening appeared in a briefing read by 300 SF residents this morning." That's a sales channel AND a PR channel.

**What NOT to do yet:** Paid ads. SEO. Product Hunt. Too early.

### Phase 2: 1,000 → 10,000 users (months 2–4)

**Unlock cities one at a time.** Don't go national — go deep on 5–10 mid-sized cities.

**The city-by-city playbook:**
1. Find the local journalist or city blogger with 5–10K followers
2. Give them a free early access account
3. Ask them to post their morning briefing card once
4. One post from a credible local voice = 50–200 signups in that city
5. That city's community posts = organic referral engine

**Referral mechanic:** "Share today's [City] top story" — one tap generates a card with the city's top headline + "Get your [City] briefing at mydailybriefing.app." Sharing feels useful (sharing news), not promotional.

**Email sequence:** 7-day onboarding sequence teaching users what the app does. Day 1: weather action item. Day 3: here's what you missed about your city council. Day 7: "invite a neighbor."

### Phase 3: 10,000+ users (months 4+)

**SEO moat:** Build static "[City] daily briefing" pages for top 500 US cities. Auto-generated from our live data. These rank for "[city name] news today" — high-intent, zero competition.

**Paid acquisition:** Only once Day-7 retention is above 50%. Don't pour money into a leaky bucket. Target: Facebook/Instagram lookalike of existing users, filtered by city. Cost: ~$2–4 CAC at scale.

**B2B play:** Sell white-label briefings to local TV stations, newspapers, and radio stations. They distribute to their audience, we get data and attribution. Revenue + growth.

## 5. Referral Loop Design

**The mechanic:** "Share your city's briefing"

After reading their briefing, users see:
> "📍 3 people from Sioux Falls joined this week. Know someone who'd want this?"
> [Share today's SF headline] → card image with headline + "Get your [city] briefing"

**Why this works:**
- Sharing news is socially normal and useful
- The city framing creates identity ("I'm a Sioux Falls person")
- The card does the selling — it's the product itself, not an ad

**Milestone referral:** After 3 referrals, unlock "Power User" tier — daily briefing delivered 15 minutes earlier, historical briefing archive.

- Post to X, Instagram Stories, Facebook

### SEO city pages

- Target: top 500 US cities by population
- These are marketing pages AND product previews — show a real live briefing for that city

### "What [City] is talking about today" series
- Weekly roundup post per city
- Pulls top community pulse stories
- Low effort, high local shareability

### X/Twitter strategy
- Post daily briefing highlights from 3–5 cities
- Engage in local city hashtags
- Partner with local accounts (we already surface them — they'll reciprocate)

## 7. Monetization Path

### Freemium model:
**Free tier:**
- Daily briefing (weather, national news, local news, basic markets)
- Up to 3 interest topics
- 1 city

**Pro tier ($4.99/month or $39/year):**
- Unlimited interest topics + sports teams + stocks
- Hyper-local layer (civic, business, events, alerts)
- Briefing archive (past 30 days)
- Email delivery at custom time
- Shareable briefing card

**Local advertising (the real upside):**
- Sell "Local Spotlight" slots in the briefing — a local business in your ZIP gets one line in your morning briefing
- Charge local businesses $199–499/month for this slot
- 500 cities × 3 advertisers each = $300K–750K MRR at scale
- This is Nextdoor's model — hyperlocal ad inventory is worth 3-5x national CPMs
- The pitch: "Your restaurant appears in the morning briefing of 800 Sioux Falls residents every day"

## 10. The Counterintuitive Bets

1. **Go smaller, not bigger.** Sioux Falls before New York. Mid-sized cities have stronger local identity and zero competition from national media.

2. **The briefing IS the marketing.** Don't run ads — post the briefing publicly every day. The content sells itself.

3. **Local businesses are the sales team.** When a local restaurant appears in your briefing, they tell their customers. Free acquisition.

4. **Don't build features — build cities.** A great briefing for 10 cities beats a mediocre briefing for 50.

5. **The local ad model is worth 10x the subscription model.** Focus subscription revenue on funding local ad inventory quality — the real prize is the ad business.

---

*Top insight: The local layer is the moat. Every competitor is nationally blind. Mid-sized cities with high local identity (Sioux Falls, Boise, Bozeman, Fargo) are the beachhead — national competitors have zero presence there, local identity is strong, and users will pay and refer. Go deep on 5 cities before going wide.*
